# Forecast stated as fact

## Statement
The tempting inadmissible interpretation writes what may happen in the present
indicative: the thing "becomes", "now depends on", "is inevitable". A forecast
is an interpretation kind of its own and is capped at level 2 however strong
its supports are, because no evidence observes the future; stated as fact, it
asserts far above that cap while looking like reporting.

## Probe
Is there a reading that states a future or conditional outcome as something
that already holds — a present-tense claim about what will follow?

## Real examples
- `reports/content_packages/a71d52f27d921529_generated.json`, generated
  2026-09-18: "your future sales and reputation now depend on whether your
  fulfillment systems can absorb the shock", written of the reader's business
  from one seller's fulfilment failure.
- `reports/content_packages/0c3ab0c08576373a_generated.json`, generated

## Conflicts
The strength ladder (`K-LAD-01`) caps a forecast at level 2 and says a forecast
is never "established". The two agree: the cap is what a forecast may assert,
and this family is how the probe finds the forecast that is not phrased as one.
