# Benefit inflation

## Statement
The tempting inadmissible interpretation turns a change in one variable into a
general gain. Money that arrives sooner becomes more money; a shorter step
becomes a cheaper process. The evidence supports the change it measured and
nothing beyond it, and the inflated reading is usually the one a reader would
most like to be true.

## Probe
Is there a reading that turns the change the evidence measured into a larger or
more general benefit — more money, more time, more customers — that the
evidence does not measure?

## Real examples
- `docs/editorial/CANONICAL_EDITORIAL_MAP_v1.md` §13 walkthrough B (Ramp,
  instant stablecoin payments): the boundary admits "the time it takes to
  receive money changes" and records the limitation "there is not more money".
  "The business has more money" is the inadmissible reading the material
  invites.

## Applies when
always

## Influences
- S-04 · `E-09.inadmissible` — the probe's finding is recorded as tested.

## Conflicts
None known. Where the evidence does measure the larger benefit, the reading is
admissible at the strength the ladder gives that evidence; this family is about
the step that is taken without measurement.
