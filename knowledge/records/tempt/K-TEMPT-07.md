# Burden inflation

## Statement
The tempting inadmissible interpretation makes the cost of a change larger than
the evidence records: a requirement becomes a doubled workload, a rule becomes
people buried in it. It is benefit inflation pointed the other way, and it is
the reading a small-business audience is most primed to accept, which is why it
has appeared in real engine output more than once.

## Probe
Is there a reading that enlarges the cost, the effort or the burden a change
imposes, beyond what the evidence records?

## Real examples
- `docs/editorial/CANONICAL_EDITORIAL_MAP_v1.md` §13 walkthrough B (Ramp): "the
  compliance checklist doubles" — the map records that this conclusion appeared
  in the real output, with no evidence of any doubling.
- `docs/editorial/CANONICAL_EDITORIAL_MAP_v1.md` §13 walkthrough C (uranium
  production): "owners were buried in inspections" — also recorded as having
  appeared in the real output, on a signal whose boundary held no admissible
  interpretation for small business at all.

## Applies when
always

## Influences
- S-04 · `E-09.inadmissible` — the probe's finding is recorded as tested.

## Conflicts
None known. A cost the evidence states is an evidence claim like any other;
this family is about the multiplier the text adds to it.

## Source
Step 2 §4 U-1, the initial probe families, from the real errors in the sample
pack and walkthroughs A–C.

## Notes
Both recorded examples come from signals where the material was thin: in
walkthrough C the correct outcome was `SKIP`, and the inflated burden was what
the engine produced instead of stopping. The probe is most useful exactly
there, which is why it applies always rather than only where a cost is
mentioned.
