# Causal overreach

## Statement
The tempting inadmissible interpretation names a cause the evidence only puts
next to an effect. Two things happened in the same period, or one was announced
before the other moved, and the reading makes the first the reason for the
second. It also covers the counterfactual: the thing that would have prevented
the outcome, which no evidence can observe.

## Probe
Is there a reading that makes one thing the cause of another, or names what
would have prevented an outcome, where the evidence records only that both
occurred?

## Real examples
- `reports/content_packages/0c3ab0c08576373a_generated.json`, generated
  2026-09-17: the source records that a traffic decline *coincided* with a
  display change, and the article states "It’s not your content. It’s not your
  customers. The culprit is the platform itself."
- `docs/editorial/CANONICAL_EDITORIAL_MAP_v1.md` §13 walkthrough A records
  "hiring would have solved the problem" as inadmissible: not in the evidence.
