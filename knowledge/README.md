# The knowledge register

*Step 4 · `docs/editorial/architecture/05_STEP4_KNOWLEDGE_REGISTER.md` (§1–§4, §7, §8)*

This is the universal knowledge the Editorial Core runs on: what the system
believes, how strong each belief is, when it applies, and when it expires. It is
plain markdown on purpose. Requirement Q1: **a person without code and without
an AI can open, understand, edit and put a record back.**

A validator (`scripts/ci/check_knowledge_register.py`) runs over this tree in CI
and again at the start of every run. A hand edit that breaks a record is
refused, and a run that starts on a broken register does not start at all — it
ends as a `SKIP` at signal scope with the reason `knowledge_register_invalid`.
That is what makes editing these files by hand safe: the worst a mistake can do
is stop a run, never change what a run publishes.

## What lives where (§1)

| Kind | Location |
|---|---|
| Universal knowledge records | `records/<family>/<K-ID>.md` |
| Probe families (`K-TEMPT-*`, U-1) | `records/tempt/<K-TEMPT-ID>.md` |
| Check records (`V-P*`, `V-T*`, `V-S*`) | `checks/<V-ID>.md` |
| Closed vocabularies | `vocab/<name>.md` |
| The universal strength ladder (U-4) | `ladders/default.md` |

`<family>` is the record id's family in lower case: `K-DST-LI-03` is filed under
`records/dst/`. **Client** rules are not here — they stay in the client's own
folder (`clients/<client>/…`), because the Client Contract is already their
authority. The register holds universal knowledge only.

The register index (`REGISTER.md`) is generated by the loader, never edited by
hand, and arrives with the loader itself.

## A knowledge record

```markdown
---
id: K-DST-LI-03
version: 2
status: candidate
tier: 4
evidence_class: VEND
confidence: low
verified_on: 2026-09-21
review_by: 2026-12-21
supersedes: K-DST-LI-03 v1
---

# External link in the body of a LinkedIn post

## Statement
One short paragraph: what the record says.

## Applies when
destination is linkedin

## Influences
S-10 · `E-14.cross_destination_link` — where the cross-destination link goes.

## Conflicts
None known.

## Source
Where it comes from.

## Change log
- v2, 2026-09-21: what changed, who, why, from which queue item
- v1, 2026-09-21: created
```

`## Notes` is optional and may hold anything a person needs. Everything else
above is required. Front-matter values come from closed lists (§2.2, §2.3):
`status` and `tier` must be an allowed pair, `approved_by` is required for an
`invariant` and for a tier-1 `approved-rule`, and `verified_on` is required for
every `K-DST-*` record and for anything classed `PLAT` or `VEND`.

**`## Applies when`** is a single line of controlled English (§4), or `always`:

```
feature documented_case is yes and not (destination is telegram)
boundary has at least 2 admissible_interpretations
strategy reveal is delayed
```

Every term must exist in `vocab/`. A term from `vocab/labels.md` is refused
outright: a record may never be conditioned on a label (I-10). That is not a
policy the parser applies — it is that the grammar has no term for a label.

**`## Influences`** is one entry per line, each beginning with the stage or
check it shapes. Entity fields are written in backticks and must exist in
`vocab/fields.md`; anything else on the line is prose for a person.

One piece of that prose is read by code, and only one: a trailing
`[fixes: <value>]` clause. A destination record that fixes one of E-14's three
mandatory values — `format`, `length_target`, `hashtags` — states the value
there, so that `src/knowledge/destination_rules.py` reads it instead of guessing
at the sentence beside it:

```
- S-10 · `E-14.length_target` — the 250–400 word range. [fixes: 250–400 words]
```

A value is written the way the engine spells it: a `format` or a `hashtags`
policy is one term of `vocab/formats.md` or of `required` / `allowed` /
`forbidden`, and a `length_target` is `<minimum>–<maximum> <unit>` with both
ends. Anything else is refused rather than interpreted. A record that shapes a
field without deciding its value carries no clause, which is a normal record:
`K-DST-FB-02` says what a Facebook caption's hashtags may be about without
deciding whether the destination carries any.

## Two destination records that are not here, and why (#363)

**`K-DST-TH-02` is deliberately not transcribed.** The map's own row reads:
"Live author replies give +42%. **Unavailable in an autonomous system** — this
is knowledge for the Client Contract, not for the production run." Transcribing
it as an engine record would import knowledge the map routes elsewhere, so the
register does not hold it. Its absence is a decision, not an oversight.

**`K-DST-META-02` and `K-DST-META-03` are transcribed unbound.** The map does
not say which destinations they bind to, and `## Applies when` cannot say "the
Meta destinations" — the six destinations are named one at a time, and grouping
three of them under a company would be the record inventing a binding. So both
are written `always`, meaning unbound, and neither reaches a destination's
`DestinationRules`: they influence S-14 and S-15 rather than S-10. **This is an
open keeper question**: whether Meta's originality and disclosure policies bind
facebook, instagram and threads is for whoever verifies the policy to state on
the record, destination by destination. `K-DST-META-01` is bound, to
`instagram`, because the map's own row names Instagram in its statement.

## A probe family (§6)

A record in `records/tempt/` with two extra required sections: `## Probe` (the
question the S-04 probe call asks) and `## Real examples` (at least one real
engine output that fell into this family). Status `descriptive` or `candidate`,
tier 3, and it influences S-04 only.

## A check record (§3)

`checks/<V-ID>.md`, with front matter `id`, `version`, `class` (`H` hard / `S`
soft), `rule_status`, `method`, `threshold`, `evidence_class`, `review_by`, and
`approved_by` where one is required. Body: `## Rule`, `## Criterion`,
`## Enforces`, `## Route`, `## Change log`, plus `## Branch criterion` for
`V-T01` and `V-T08` only.

`## Route` is a table, and every row of it is checked against the stage-topology
registry (`src/editorial_core/topology.py`), which is where the Step 2 §5.3
route table lives:

```markdown
| Finding | Route | Cause | Counter | On exhaustion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a fact is misquoted from the core | S-13 → S-12 | phrasing_or_removable_fact | L_edit | skip_publication |
| near-exact republication | terminal | — | — | skip_publication |
| the signal is below the destination norm | hint | — | — | — |
```

A soft check (`class: S`) routes nothing: its rows are `hint`, and its
`threshold` is `none` unless an owner approved one (I-12).

## The strength ladder (§7)

`ladders/default.md` is the universal ladder, as a knowledge record with a
`## Levels` table. A **client** may declare its own ladder in its contract, as
`claim_strength_ceiling`, and each of its levels must say which universal level
it maps to:

```markdown
### claim_strength_ceiling

- observed on one shop floor → universal level 2
- confirmed by the manufacturer → universal level 3
```

A client ladder may rename the universal levels and may restrict them further.
It may never permit a stronger assertion than the universal ladder allows for
the same evidence, and the validator is what makes that checkable.

## Changing anything here (§10)

Offline, by the keeper, always with a new `version` and a `## Change log` line.
Making a record an `invariant`, adding a tier-1 hard rule, or giving a soft
check a threshold needs the **owner's** approval, recorded in `approved_by`.
A new vocabulary term needs a matching code change: the term and its meaning
ship together, and until they do the validator refuses every record using it.

Platform metrics never change a file. They create queue items, and the keeper
decides.
