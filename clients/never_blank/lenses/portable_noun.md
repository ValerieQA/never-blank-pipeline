Every article is evaluated for a portable noun. The decision is one of three,
and it is recorded with its reason:

- **mint** — we name the thing ourselves, because this article establishes
  something that has no good name yet;
- **reuse** — we use a term Never Blank has already minted, because this
  article is another case of the same thing;
- **none** — the material does not carry one.

None is a legitimate outcome and is preferred over a weak coinage. A noun
minted to satisfy a checklist is a machine tell.

**The cut test, for minting.** The term must be defined in one sentence that
survives being cut out of the article: lift that sentence, read it alone, and
it must still say what the thing is. If the definition only makes sense in
place, the term is not portable — write the article without it.

**Never borrow somebody else's coinage as ours.** Another publication's term
may be quoted, attributed and discussed. It may never be presented as Never
Blank's own, and it may never be the noun this article hands the reader.

A reused term keeps the meaning it was minted with. Where this article would
stretch it, mint a new one or use none.
