# Never Blank Wednesday Golden Contract

## Purpose

Wednesday is the Never Blank **signal interpretation** product. It is not a generic news recap, a list of lessons, or a Monday-style business case.

The Wednesday job is:

```text
raw event
→ obvious reading
→ turn
→ hidden / second-order meaning
→ mechanism
→ evidence
→ business implication
→ bounded question
→ Never Blank Echo
```

The reader should receive one coherent article. The labels below are **internal reasoning/writing layers** and should not appear as section headings in the published article unless explicitly chosen editorially.

---

## End-to-end Wednesday layers

### 1. Discovery — find broad candidate signals

Wednesday searches broad business/news sources for events that may contain a non-obvious business signal.

Historical discovery philosophy:

> Be inclusive — prefer false positives over missed signals.

Wednesday may consider large companies, M&A, technology, capital allocation, market shifts, strategic moves, and other events that would be rejected by a Monday-specific owner-led/SMB rule.

The purpose of discovery is **recall**, not premature narrowing.

### 2. Selection — choose a signal worth interpreting

A candidate is valuable when it has enough substance to support a deeper interpretation, not merely because it is a large headline.

Selection should favor signals with:

- a concrete event;
- an observable tension or contradiction;
- a plausible second-order meaning;
- enough source material to support factual grounding;
- business relevance beyond the headline itself.

Selection must not require perfect evidence before a signal is allowed to proceed.

### 3. Enrichment — understand what happened

Carry forward the strongest available factual/business context, including concepts such as:

- CORE_FACT
- WHY_IT_MATTERS_TO_BUSINESS
- BUSINESS_RESPONSES_OBSERVED
- REAL_COMPANY_EXAMPLE
- PROBLEM_FACED
- RESPONSE_TAKEN
- OUTCOME_IF_KNOWN
- SOURCE_FOR_CASE
- BUSINESS_LESSON
- DID_IT_WORK
- EVIDENCE_OF_OUTCOME
- TIME_HORIZON
- COUNTER_EXAMPLE
- WHY_THIS_CASE_IS_INTERESTING
- CORE_TENSION

This stage should provide raw material for interpretation; it does not itself replace the Never Blank angle.

### 4. Angle generation — find the hidden meaning

The central Wednesday transformation is:

```text
raw event
→ hidden meaning / second-order interpretation
→ tension / mechanism
→ business implication
→ strong hook
→ Never Blank angle
```

Relevant angle concepts include:

- LINKEDIN_ANGLE
- BLOG_ANGLE
- POTENTIAL_HOOK
- INTERESTING_QUESTION
- NEVER_BLANK_ANGLE
- POSSIBLE_SIGNATURE_LINE
- TARGET_AUDIENCE
- PRIMARY_CHANNEL

The first plausible lesson is not automatically the Never Blank angle. Wednesday should continue reasoning until it finds the non-obvious interpretation that makes the event worth publishing.

### 5. Retrieval / evidence readiness — verify the selected signal

After selection, retrieve and verify the original source material needed to support the article.

This stage exists to ground claims. It should not become an unbounded search loop.

If the selected signal is fundamentally supportable, proceed with the strongest verified evidence available. If evidence supports only a narrower claim, narrow the claim rather than cycling through candidates indefinitely.

### 6. Article generation — follow the Golden article movement

The article must move intellectually. Each major section should advance the interpretation rather than restate the same conclusion.

#### Layer A — EVENT
State the concrete event.

Question answered:

> What happened?

#### Layer B — OBVIOUS X
State the obvious or conventional reading that most readers could make from the headline.

Question answered:

> What would everyone naturally assume this means?

#### Layer C — TURN
Introduce the contradiction, timing problem, mismatch, or detail that makes the obvious reading incomplete.

Question answered:

> What does not fit the obvious explanation?

#### Layer D — HIDDEN Y
State the non-obvious second-order meaning.

This is the core Never Blank move.

Question answered:

> If the obvious reading is incomplete, what is this event actually telling us?

#### Layer E — MECHANISM
Explain why the hidden meaning is plausible. The article should identify the underlying business mechanism rather than merely restating Hidden Y.

Question answered:

> Why does this happen?

#### Layer F — EVIDENCE
**Show the research; do not summarize it.**

Evidence must use the strongest concrete facts already available in the researched and verified source material to demonstrate why the interpretation is credible.

Prefer:

- specific numbers;
- dates;
- reported actions;
- before/after comparisons;
- timelines;
- observable outcomes;
- explicitly reported management or market statements when relevant.

When claiming a relationship between an action and an outcome, show the observable sequence whenever the research supports it:

```text
what happened
→ when it happened
→ what changed afterward
```

Do not replace concrete evidence with abstract language such as:

- “improved performance”;
- “greater confidence”;
- “disciplined progress”;
- “the market rewarded the company”;

when the source material contains more specific facts.

A reviewer should be able to ask:

> Where exactly in the source is this fact?

and receive a concrete answer.

#### Evidence is supporting material, not a new selection gate

Evidence supports the selected signal; it does not control discovery or restart selection by default.

Do **not**:

- reject an otherwise strong Wednesday signal merely because evidence is not perfect;
- restart discovery solely to obtain a prettier Evidence paragraph;
- cycle through additional candidates indefinitely;
- initiate unbounded extra research;
- spend additional provider/LLM budget just to strengthen wording.

Instead:

```text
if evidence is narrower than the interpretation
→ narrow the claim
→ keep the signal when it remains supportable
```

Never imply causation when the material establishes only timing, correlation, or commentary. If the evidence proves only sequence, describe sequence. If it supports correlation, say correlation. If causation is not established, do not manufacture it.

#### Layer G — BUSINESS IMPLICATION
Move beyond the individual company/event without flattening the insight into generic advice.

Question answered:

> What broader business implication follows specifically from this mechanism?

The implication must arise from the article's actual Hidden Y + Mechanism, not from a generic coaching template.

#### Layer H — BOUNDED BUSINESS QUESTION
Ask a specific question that follows naturally from the mechanism and implication.

Avoid generic AI-coaching closes such as:

> “Reflect on how your business communicates progress.”

Prefer a question that could only have been generated from this article's actual reasoning.

#### Layer I — NEVER BLANK ECHO
Close with a short, ownable Never Blank thought that compresses the article's interpretation.

The Echo should not merely repeat the hook or restate the same sentence for a final time.

## Anti-flattening rules

Wednesday must not collapse into:

- a news recap;
- “X is important because X is important” repetition;
- a generic “growth vs profitability” lesson when a deeper interpretation exists;
- a listicle;
- a generic business-coaching CTA;
- multiple paragraphs restating the same first insight;
- unsupported causal claims;
- evidence written as abstract interpretation.

A good Wednesday article should demonstrate **intellectual movement**.

A useful self-check:

```text
Does each major paragraph add a new step in the reasoning?
```

If several paragraphs can be summarized by the same sentence, the article has flattened.

## ChargePoint example — intended reasoning path

A valid reasoning path for the ChargePoint signal is:

```text
EVENT
ChargePoint shares rose about 70%.

OBVIOUS X
The market renewed its confidence in EV charging / ChargePoint growth.

TURN
ChargePoint had been expanding its network for years; that expansion itself did not create the same reaction.

HIDDEN Y
The market changed what it was willing to count as evidence of progress.

MECHANISM
In a capital-intensive growth market, scale can initially function as proof of future value. Eventually, added scale stops answering the economic question; investors require evidence that accumulated scale can improve business economics.

EVIDENCE
Use concrete researched facts: exact reported revenue/loss/guidance figures, dates, and the observable timing of the share move when those facts are available. Do not substitute “revenue improved” or “confidence increased” for the actual research.

BUSINESS IMPLICATION
A company can continue progressing operationally while the metric it uses to demonstrate progress has become obsolete in the eyes of its audience.

BOUNDED QUESTION
Is the metric you are emphasizing still what your audience considers proof of progress?

ECHO
Sometimes the strategy has not stopped working. The definition of progress has changed.
```

This example illustrates the contract; it does not pin exact probabilistic wording.

## Product invariant

Wednesday is successful only when it preserves both sides of the product:

```text
research / evidence discipline
+
non-obvious Never Blank interpretation
```

Safety, acceptance, source transparency, and evidence verification do not replace editorial intelligence. Editorial intelligence does not relax factual grounding. Both are required.
