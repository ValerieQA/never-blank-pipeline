# Never Blank Editorial Style

## Core Principle

Never Blank does not explain events.

Never Blank studies how businesses respond to events.

Bad:

Signal → Opinion

Good:

Signal → Tension → Response → Outcome → Lesson

---

## Audience

Primary:

- founders
- business owners
- consultants
- service businesses
- agencies
- small teams

Not:

- HR audience
- investors
- general news audience

---

## What Makes a Good Never Blank Article

A strong article contains:

1. Signal
2. Real business response
3. Real company example
4. Observable outcome
5. Business lesson
6. Never Blank signature

---

## Preferred Formula

Signal

↓

What businesses are actually doing

↓

Real company example

↓

Outcome

↓

Business lesson

↓

Never Blank signature

↓

Source

---

## Writing Rules

Do not explain obvious facts.

Do not teach basic concepts.

Do not define inflation.

Do not define AI.

Do not define marketing.

Assume reader is already a business owner.

Every paragraph must add a new observation.

If a sentence can be deleted without losing information, remove it.

---

## Case Studies Are Mandatory

A signal without a company example is incomplete.

The reader should be able to answer:

"Who actually did this?"

before the article ends.

---

## Tension First

Every article should identify the hidden business tension.

Examples:

- Trust vs Margin
- Growth vs Efficiency
- AI Adoption vs Headcount
- Price Increase vs Retention
- Visibility vs Operational Load

The article is about the tension, not the headline.

---

## Never Blank Signature

Never Blank should occupy less than 10% of article space.

Never Blank is not the article.

Never Blank is the observation that emerges from the article.

Examples:

> Never Blank: Watch the structure change, not the headcount.

> Never Blank: The trade-off is the story.

> Never Blank: The signal is rarely the event itself.

---

## Social Channel Adaptation

**LinkedIn:** Observation + business lesson.

**Blog:** Signal + case study + outcome + lesson.

**Threads:** Provocative observation.

**Stories:** Question or tension.

---

## Editorial Test

Before publishing ask:

Did the reader learn:

- a signal?
- a real response?
- a real example?
- a business lesson?

If not, rewrite.
